# De-slopping AI-Generated Content

Rules for any pipeline or agent that emits human-readable copy in French or
English. If a draft fails this file, regenerate or rewrite — do not ship.

## Why this matters

Merriam-Webster named "slop" its 2025 Word of the Year. Readers,
journalists, and Google's helpful-content systems all pattern-match on the
same tells within the first paragraph. Generic AI copy reads as a cheap
template — it kills trust on a landing page and tanks dwell time on a blog
post. Credibility is the opposite of slop.

## Banned vocabulary

A single instance can be defensible. **Two or more from this list in the same
piece = rewrite.** Cluster density is the tell, not isolated words.

### Filler verbs (FR / EN)
- plonger / delve, dive into
- exploiter, tirer parti de / leverage, harness, utilize
- favoriser, encourager / foster, empower, ignite
- rationaliser, optimiser / streamline, optimize
- mettre en lumière, souligner / underscore, highlight, showcase
- naviguer (au figuré) / navigate (figurative)
- débloquer, libérer / unlock, unleash
- révolutionner, transformer (sans preuve) / revolutionize, transform
- faciliter, délivrer / facilitate, deliver (used corporate-style)

### Inflated adjectives
- robuste, pointe, dernier cri, de pointe / robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art
- transparent, fluide, sans accroc / seamless
- crucial, essentiel, pivotal, primordial / crucial, pivotal, essential
- innovant, révolutionnaire, disruptif / innovative, revolutionary, disruptive
- dynamique, polyvalent, multifacette / dynamic, multifaceted, versatile
- complet, exhaustif (sans contenu derrière) / comprehensive
- vibrant, profond / vibrant, profound

### Abstract container nouns
- paysage, écosystème, royaume, sphère, domaine / landscape, realm, ecosystem, sphere
- tapisserie, mosaïque, symphonie, voyage / tapestry, mosaic, symphony, journey
- témoignage de / testament to
- synergie, méthodologie, mise en œuvre / synergy, methodology, implementation
- effet de levier, valeur ajoutée / leverage (n.), value-add

### Hedges and throat-clearing
- "il est important de noter que" / "it's important to note that"
- "il convient de souligner que" / "it's worth noting that"
- "on pourrait argumenter que" / "it could be argued that"
- "d'une manière générale" / "generally speaking"
- "vise à" / "aims to"
- "dans cet article, nous allons" / "in this article we will"

### Transition clichés
- en outre, par ailleurs, de plus, néanmoins, c'est pourquoi (when stacked) / furthermore, moreover, additionally, however
- en conclusion, en somme, en définitive / in conclusion, in essence, ultimately
- au bout du compte, en fin de journée / at the end of the day

### Opener / closer clichés
- "Dans le monde actuel en constante évolution…" / "In today's fast-paced world…"
- "À l'ère du numérique…" / "In the digital age…"
- "Imaginez un instant…" / "Picture this…"
- "En tant que [profession], vous savez que…" / "As a [role], you know…"
- "Plongeons dans le sujet" / "Let's dive in"

## Banned structures

### 1. The "Not X, but Y" contrastive reframe
This is the loudest 2026 tell. Kill on sight.

- Bad: "Ce n'est pas juste un site web, c'est votre vitrine numérique."
- Bad: "We don't just build sites — we craft digital experiences."
- Good: "Un site qui amène des clients en boutique."
- Good: "We build sites. They bring you customers."

### 2. Avoidance of "est / is"
AI swaps simple copulas for inflated verbs. Use "est/is" when it's the
truth.

- Bad: "Notre boulangerie incarne l'art du pain artisanal."
- Good: "Notre boulangerie fait du pain au levain, cuit le matin."

### 3. Rule of three / tricolon abuse
One tricolon per piece, max. Two and the rhythm becomes a metronome.

- Bad: "Rapide, fiable, et professionnel. Local, accessible, et chaleureux."
- Good: "Rapide et fiable. L'équipe connaît votre quartier."

### 4. Listicle-everything / bolded bullet headers
Bullets are for genuine enumerations (opening hours, services, prices). They
are not the default container for prose. **No "**Bold lead-in:** explanation"
bullet patterns** — those are the generic LLM formatting signature.

- Blog posts: aim for ≤ 1 bulleted list per 800 words, only when the content
  is genuinely a list (steps, prices, requirements).
- Landing-page service blocks: short noun phrases ("Plomberie d'urgence
  24/7"), not bolded-header explanation patterns.

### 5. Em-dash overuse
Humans use 0-2 em dashes per 500 words. AI uses 5-10. Replace most with
periods, commas, or parentheses. Never use em dashes for "dramatic pauses."

### 6. Predictable parallelism
AI loves perfectly parallel grammar across bullets and sentences. Break it
deliberately — vary length, verb form, structure.

- Bad: "Augmenter votre visibilité. Améliorer votre image. Convertir vos visiteurs."
- Good: "Plus de visibilité sur Google. Une image qui inspire confiance. Et des visiteurs qui appellent."

### 7. The "Challenges and Future Prospects" closer
Vague speculation paragraph at the end about evolving landscapes / future
possibilities. Cut it. End on a concrete next step or a specific claim.

### 8. Faux-empathy openers
"Picture this," "Imaginez," "As a [profession], you know that…" — delete and
start with a specific fact or scene.

### 9. Uniform sentence length
Burstiness is the strongest statistical AI tell. Mix 4-word sentences with
25-word sentences. Use fragments. Sometimes one word.

### 10. Throat-clearing first paragraph
AI almost always opens with a generic setup paragraph before the actual
content. Cut the first paragraph of any AI draft and check if the piece is
better. Usually it is.

## Positive rules

### Concrete beats abstract
Every paragraph should contain at least one of: a number, a proper noun, a
place name, a date, a price, a measurable outcome, or a named object. If a
paragraph contains zero of these, it's filler.

- Bad: "Nous offrons des services de plomberie de qualité dans votre région."
- Good: "Dépannage fuite ou chauffe-eau à Lyon 3e, intervention sous 2h, devis fixe avant travaux."

### Verbs over abstract nouns
French and English both: prefer a verb to an "-tion / -ment / -ity" noun.

- Bad: "L'optimisation de votre présence digitale" → Good: "Vous trouver sur Google"
- Bad: "Implementation of best practices" → Good: "We do X, Y, Z"

### Take a stance
A piece without an opinion reads as AI. Pick a side. Recommend one option.
Disagree with a common belief. Name a thing as bad.

- Bad: "Il existe plusieurs approches pour le référencement local."
- Good: "Pour un café de quartier, Google Business Profile compte plus que le SEO classique. Concentrez-vous là d'abord."

### Primary evidence
For blog posts, every claim that isn't obvious should anchor to: a named
example (real bakery, real plumber, real café), a number you can defend, a
named tool / law / platform, or a quote. No vague "studies show."

### Cut the first sentence of every paragraph
After drafting, audit each paragraph: is the first sentence throat-clearing?
Most AI first sentences are. Delete it. The paragraph usually still works.

### Voice consistency
Pick one register at the top and do not drift. No piece may mix. For French,
that usually means: marketing copy = "vous", blog content = "tu". For
English, pick a single level of formality and hold it.

### One idea per sentence (mostly), then break the rule
Default to one clean idea per sentence. Then deliberately fuse two into a
long, comma-rich sentence once per 4-6 sentences to break the rhythm.

### Plain words over technical jargon
Say "ChatGPT" not "GEO" or "LLM". Say "votre site sur Google" not "votre
présence digitale". Say "le cadenas dans le navigateur" not "le certificat
SSL/TLS". Translate every acronym and industry term into the words a real
reader uses.

### Title case is forbidden in French
French headings use sentence case: capitalize only the first word and proper
nouns. "Comment optimiser votre fiche Google" — not "Comment Optimiser Votre
Fiche Google". Title case in French is itself an AI tell.

## French-specific

### Anglicisms AI overuses
Replace these with French equivalents:
- "leverager" → "utiliser"
- "implémenter" → "mettre en place"
- "deliverer" → "livrer", "fournir"
- "scaler" → "passer à l'échelle"
- "process" (nom) → "procédure", "démarche"
- "best practices" → "bonnes pratiques"

### French-specific AI tics
- "Dans un monde en constante évolution" — banned.
- "Il est crucial de" — banned, use "Il faut" or just say the thing.
- "En constante évolution" — banned full stop.
- "Une tapisserie complexe de…" — banned.
- "Naviguer dans le paysage de…" — banned.

### Curly quotes and typography
French quotation marks are guillemets « » with non-breaking spaces. AI
defaults to straight "..." or English "...". Using the wrong quote style is
a visible tell to French readers. Use « » for French; use "" only inside
code or when quoting English.

### Contractions and register
French copy aimed at small-business readers should feel spoken. Use "c'est",
"y'a" sparingly in blog (tu) content. In marketing (vous) content, allow
"On" as the subject where natural — AI defaults to "Nous" everywhere, which
reads stiff.

## Self-check before output

Before returning a draft, the LLM runs this checklist and rewrites until all
pass. If any fail twice, surface the failure rather than ship.

1. **Cluster check**: search the draft for the banned-vocabulary list. ≤ 1
   hit total per 500 words. ≥ 2 hits = rewrite.
2. **Em-dash count**: ≤ 2 per 500 words. Over = replace.
3. **"Not X, but Y" / "It's not X — it's Y" patterns**: zero. Search the
   draft, delete every instance.
4. **Concrete-noun density**: every paragraph contains a number, name,
   place, price, date, or specific object. If not, rewrite or delete.
5. **First sentence of every paragraph**: not throat-clearing. If it could
   open any blog post on any topic, rewrite.
6. **Opening hook**: not "Dans le monde", "À l'ère", "Imaginez",
   "Picture this", "In today's". If yes, rewrite from a specific fact.
7. **Bullet density**: blog post ≤ 1 bulleted list per ~800 words.
   Landing page only uses bullets for real enumerations. No "**Bold:**
   explanation" bullet patterns anywhere.
8. **Sentence-length variance**: not all sentences within 12-20 words.
   Include at least one ≤ 6 words and one ≥ 25 words per 400-word block.
9. **Stance**: the piece recommends, prefers, or rejects something
   specific. A neutral both-sides survey = rewrite.
10. **Voice consistency**: one register, held throughout. For French:
    "vous" OR "tu", never both.
11. **French typography**: guillemets « » not "", sentence case in
    headings, no "implémenter / leverager / process" anglicisms.
12. **Closer**: ends on a concrete next step, price, phone number, or
    specific claim. No "challenges and future prospects" vagueness, no
    "en conclusion".

If the LLM cannot pass all 12 after two rewrite passes, return the draft
flagged `[DESLOPPING FAILED: <which checks>]` so a human operator can
intervene. Do not ship slop silently.
